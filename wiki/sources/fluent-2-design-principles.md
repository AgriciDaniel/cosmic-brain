# Source: Fluent 2 Design Principles

**URL**: https://fluent2.microsoft.design/design-principles
**Fetched**: 2026-05-24
**System**: Fluent 2 Design System (Microsoft)

## Summary

The four foundational principles of Microsoft's Fluent 2 Design System. Each principle pairs a **functional aspect** (what it does for the product) with an **emotional aspect** (what it does for the user). The principles express shared vision and guide decision-making across all Fluent surfaces — including the implementations in [[FluentUI Blazor]] and the broader Fluent component libraries.

## The Four Principles

| Principle | User intent | Functional | Emotional |
|-----------|-------------|------------|-----------|
| **Natural on Every Platform** | "I want to know what to do." | Adapt to device + platform; reuse native components ~80% of the time | Intuitive, expected → reliability and trust |
| **Built for Focus** | "I want to stay in the flow." | Tech communicates and performs without obstructing user goals | Reduced clutter → calm, centered, confident |
| **One for All, All for One** | "I want to be included." | Diverse perspectives included early; constraints drive creativity | Inclusion → belonging |
| **Unmistakably Microsoft** | "I want to recognize what I'm looking for." | Signature experiences tied to MS identity via color, sound, illustration, icons | Personality → connection and recognition |

## Key Points

- Principles are **values**, not rules. They guide decision-making rather than prescribe outcomes.
- The 80% number under "Natural on Every Platform" is a deliberate stance: most experiences should ride on native platform conventions; the remaining 20% is where signature/differentiating work happens.
- Inclusion is framed as a **design input** (perspectives shape solutions), not just an accessibility checkbox.
- "Unmistakably Microsoft" elevates **multi-sensory** identity — color, sound, illustration, and icons together — rather than logo placement alone.
