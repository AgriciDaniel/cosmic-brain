# Types of AI Harm - Fluent 2 Design System

## Overview

AI systems can generate outputs or behaviors that harm users, organizations, or society. Harm may be unintentional but still damages trust and creates real-world consequences.

## Harm Categories

- **Inaccurate** — factually incorrect, fabricated, misleading
- **Incomplete** — omitted critical info, unfinished tasks
- **Biased** — reinforces unfair assumptions (identity, group, culture)
- **Inappropriate or unsafe** — offensive, harmful, discriminatory, dangerous
- **Non-transparent** — fails to disclose AI involvement, data sources, constraints
- **Overreliance** — users accept AI output without verification because UX failed

## Detailed Harm Types

### Inaccurate
Hallucinated citations, misleading summaries. High stakes: health, legal, financial.

**Mitigations**: approved disclaimer ("AI-generated content may be incorrect. Check for accuracy."), surface sources, UI friction before action.

### Incomplete
Omits critical info, stops short, technically correct but missing context.

**Mitigations**: scope task boundaries, progressive disclosure for limitations, communicate partial/capability-constrained responses.

### Biased
Reflects/amplifies unfair assumptions. Inherited from training data at scale; often invisible in individual outputs.

**Mitigations**: inclusive content reviews, test across diverse populations, feedback mechanisms for bias.

### Inappropriate / Unsafe
Offensive, harmful, discriminatory. Requires model + UI safeguards.

**Mitigations**: feedback categorization "Unsafe or problematic content"; clear escalation pathways.

### Non-transparent
Users unaware of AI involvement, sources, insufficient disclosure.

**Mitigations**: label AI content consistently, surface sources inline, approved disclaimer language, communicate scope at entry points.

### Overreliance
Users trust outputs without critical thinking — UI failed to communicate uncertainty/prompt review/make verification easy.

**Mitigations**: surface accuracy caveats inline, clear source links, avoid false-authority patterns, friction points for review.

## Feedback and Harm Reporting

Categories: Output wasn't factual · Incomplete output · No sources provided · Unsafe or problematic content · Questionable sources · Other.

Feedback collection requires transparency: "When you submit feedback, the prompt and response are included" — not "Help us improve."

**Critical**: Scoring 0/1 on "Prevent overreliance" = automatic failure.
