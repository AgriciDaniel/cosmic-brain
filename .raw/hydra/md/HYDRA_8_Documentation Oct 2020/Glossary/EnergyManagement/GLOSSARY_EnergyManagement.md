Definitions

Consumption

Consumption means the withdrawal of raw material and semi-finished products for the

production  process.  They  are  consumed  by  the  process.  Such  consumption  also

includes  energy  consumption  in  any  form:  electric  energy,  gas,  oil,  compressed  air,

water in turbines, etc.

Since  energy  actually  cannot  be  consumed,  this  is  indeed  more  precisely  called

energy demand.

The  consumption  in  HYDRA  is  "measured",  in  kWh,  m3,  l  or  other  quantities

convertible to joule, according to the relevant medium.

Note: The colloquial expression "to consume current" is technically not correct, since

the current supplied to a device  will leave it again (principle of charge conservation).

What  actually  "flows"  is  electric  energy!  However,  this  is  not  in  fact  consumed  but

converted  according  to  the  principle  of  energy  conservation.  The  work  performed  in

this  process  (the  product  of  voltage,  amperage  and  time  in  the  case  of  ohmic

recipients)  is  measured  by  a  so-called  electric  meter  and  hence  represents  the

consumption of electric energy.

Electric work

Electric  work  is  the  physical  term  for  the  transmitted  quantity  of  electric  energy  and

hence synonymous for the energy industry. This means the work specifies the energy

demand.  The  unit  of  electric  work  can  generally  be  indicated  in  joule,  "J".  In  electric

energy engineering, the unit "kWh" is used. Conversion: (1 kWh corresponds to 3600

kJ).

The electric meters connected in HYDRA directly supply the electric work and hence

the current "consumption".

Energy demand  Amount  of  energy  to  be  put  through  for  work  performance  -  please  also  refer  to

"Electric work" or "Consumption". The energy demand is frequently related to a period,

e.g.  a  year.  In  statistics,  the  primary  energy  demand  is,  for  example,  indicated  in  PJ

(petajoule).  Both  electric  energy  and  energy  generated  by  other  means  is  measured

by  this.  In  Germany,  for  example,  14,000.00  PJ  of  energy  were  required  in  2010

(approx. 3,900 bn kWh).

Energy

Energy is a physical quantity identified by the symbol "E". Energy may be measured in

a  variety  of  units,  depending  on  the  application.  Since  energy  is  related  to  the  work

performed  for  energy  conversion,  both  energy  and  work  may  be  indicated  in  joule,

kWh, Nm, eV, or even in kg (according to Einstein). The electric energy is calculated

from U*I*t, i.e. the product of amperage, voltage and time. In addition, energy and/or

work  is  the  integral  of  capacity  in  mathematical  terms.  This  also  explains  the

connection  between  capacity  meters  and  consumption  meters!  According  to  the

principle  of  energy  conservation,  energy  is  not  consumed  but  only  converted.  The

required work corresponds to the amount of converted energy.

Current

In technical terminology, "current" is the designation  of its strength. More precisely, it

is  the  physical  quantity  "amperage"  designated  by  the  symbol  I  and  the  unit  ampere

(A). In colloquial language, it  is generally used to specify the transmission of electric

energy,  which  tends  to  result  in  confusion!  Amperage  thus  describes  the  amount  of

electric charge transported in a specific period of time.

In the context of HYDRA, current is a measured physical process quantity expressed

in  ampere,  i.e.  it  is  recorded  by  the  mechanisms  of  the  PDV  integrated  in  the  EMG

module.

Electric meter

The  electric  meter  measures  the  electric  work  determined  by  the  connected

consumers!  This  means  the  energy  flow.  The  unit  counted  by  the  electric  meter  is

kWh, i.e. "kilowatt hours". Some devices also provide power values.

Voltage

Electric  voltage  is  a  technical  quantity  and  is  measured  in  Volt  (V).  It  describes  the

potential difference of electric charge between two conductors and is hence practically

the power for the electric current, or, more precisely, the work capacity of the electric

charge  in  the  electric  field.  Regarding  the  specification,  a  distinction  is  to  be  made

between  constant  direct  current  voltage  and  alternating  voltage,  where  voltage

changes sinusoidally over time. The domestic mains supply in Germany operates with

220 - 230 V (effective value) AC. As from 50 V AC, the insulation capacity of dry skin

is  overcome  so  that  contact  causing  ventricular  fibrillation  may  be  lethal.  For  more

information, please refer to the relevant literature from physics, electrical engineering

and medicine.

Capacity

Capacity is a physical quantity which, in simple terms, is calculated as the quotient of

work  (or  expended  energy)  per  unit  of  time.  The  unit  for  capacity  is  Watt  ("W")  or

multiples  of  it.  Capacity  may  be  indicated  for  consumers  as  for  energy  generators

(megawatt  for  power  stations).  The  instantaneous  capacity  or  power  means  the

energy  demand  when  the  time  interval  moves  towards  zero.  In  mathematical  terms,

this is calculated as differential quotient or 1st derivative. As regards electric capacity,

the  capacity  is  calculated  as  the  product  of  voltage  and  amperage  (U*I).  This

illustrates the relationship between energy and capacity. Since alternating currents are

often  used  in  energy  engineering,  and  because  this  value  is  a  quantity  fluctuating

across  the  phases,  a  mean  value  is  obtained;  this  is  then  called  effective  power,

reactive  power  or  apparent  power.  The  effective  power  is  the  capacity  which  can  be

used by consumers.

In HYDRA, the capacity is measured by connected technical recording devices, in the

same way as the amperage or voltage, for example.

As  regards  non-electrical  energy  sources,  the  consumption  per  unit  of  time  can  be

measured  as  capacity,  e.g.  by  means  of  flow-through  scales  and/or  counters,  or  by

"speed-measuring" sensors.

Since an average capacity over a defined period can be calculated from consumption

very easily, it is possible to convert consumption figures into capacities in HYDRA.

Energy efficiency Key  figure  for  measuring  the  specific  energy  demand  per  utility.  The  key  figures  are

defined  individually  per  company  or  product,  since  the  utility  has  to  be  defined

differently  in  each  case.  Energy  efficiency  can,  for  example,  be  measured  in  energy

demand  per  unit  produced.  In  contrast  to  this,  the  efficiency  is  a  purely  technical

quantity and describes the quotient from energy emitted to energy supplied.
